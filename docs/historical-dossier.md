# Historical Character Design Dossier: Kings and Generals for a Strategy Game

## Research frame and recommended interpretation

The supplied roster contains twelve historical figures: **Han Xin, Lê Thánh Tông, Lý Thường Kiệt, Minh Mạng/Minh Mệnh, Napoleon Bonaparte, Nguyễn Ánh/Gia Long, Nguyễn Huệ/Quang Trung, Nguyễn Phúc Chu, Trần Hưng Đạo, Trần Nhân Tông, Trịnh Cương, and Xiang Yu**. They are not all comparable in the same way. Some were extraordinary field commanders, some were rulers whose main strength was institution-building, and several combined political and military leadership. Treating every one of them as a conventional battlefield “general” would therefore flatten the history and make the game roster less interesting.

For game design, the most useful distinction is between **historically evidenced capability** and **gameplay translation**. For example, Han Xin's maneuver warfare is well attested in the traditional accounts of the Chu–Han conflict, whereas assigning him a “10/10 Maneuver” statistic is a designer's interpretation rather than a historical measurement. The same applies to judging weaknesses: a ruler's structural problem—succession, logistics, frontier administration, coalition management—should not automatically be converted into a claim that the person was personally incompetent.

A particularly important caution concerns ancient Chinese campaigns. Han Xin and Xiang Yu's careers are known primarily through the classical historiographical tradition associated with texts such as Sima Qian's *Shiji*; dramatic troop totals and some anecdotes should therefore be treated as literary-historical traditions rather than modern audited battlefield statistics. The surviving tradition nevertheless gives unusually clear evidence for contrasting command styles: Han Xin repeatedly used maneuver, deception and psychological manipulation, whereas Xiang Yu became famous for aggressive battle leadership and extraordinary battlefield shock but failed to turn battlefield dominance into durable political supremacy. citeturn18view2turn17search0turn17search9

The Vietnamese cases require similar care. Modern museum, heritage and commemorative accounts preserve important information, but interpretations are strongest when cross-checked against academic studies from sources such as Cambridge University Press and UNESCO. This is especially important for famous episodes such as Bạch Đằng, Nguyễn Huệ's 1789 campaign, and the institutional reforms of the Lê, Nguyễn and Trịnh governments. citeturn11search12turn13search1turn15search13turn18view0

### Roster identity and best game archetype

| Character | Historical identity | Best gameplay identity | Core fantasy |

| **Xiang Yu** | Chu warlord and battlefield commander | Shock / morale general | Overwhelm enemies through terrifying offensive power |
| **Napoleon Bonaparte** | Emperor and operational commander | Corps / concentration general | Move separately, concentrate rapidly, destroy armies |
| **Lý Thường Kiệt** | Lý commander and statesman | Preemption / fortified-defense general | Strike first, then force the enemy into a prepared defense |

That differentiation is historically more defensible—and likely more enjoyable—than making all twelve variations of an “attack +20%” commander.

## Battlefield commanders and their strengths

**Han Xin — the maneuver specialist**

Han Xin's historical reputation rests less on personal combat than on manipulating the enemy's expectations. In the campaign against Wei, the traditional account describes him creating the impression that his main crossing would occur at one location while sending his actual striking force by another route. At Jingxing against Zhao, he placed troops with their backs toward a river while detaching light cavalry to infiltrate and seize the enemy camp once the Zhao army had been drawn out. The Zhao command also rejected Li Zuoche's proposal to threaten Han Xin's supply route—exactly the sort of vulnerability Han Xin himself was adept at exploiting. These episodes explain why later tradition made him an archetype of indirect maneuver and deceptive command. citeturn18view2turn8search38

His strongest historical characteristics are therefore **operational deception, reading enemy psychology, divided-force coordination, turning apparent weakness into a trap, and operating successfully while apparently disadvantaged**. He should be one of the game's best generals when the player understands terrain, scouting and enemy behavior rather than simply having the biggest army. citeturn18view2turn8search38

His great weakness was political rather than tactical. After the Han victory, his extraordinary prestige and previous independent commands made him vulnerable within Liu Bang's new political order; he was successively reduced in status and ultimately executed. Whatever one concludes about the precise conspiracy accusations, the career trajectory demonstrates a fundamental contrast between his ability to win campaigns and his inability to secure an equally durable position at court. citeturn18view2

**Game translation:** Han Xin should have perhaps the highest **Maneuver** and **Deception** ratings in the roster but unusually poor **Court Security**. Winning too many independent victories could accumulate “Renown,” which increases military power while simultaneously increasing ruler suspicion. This creates historically inspired strategic tension instead of an arbitrary stat penalty.

A signature skill called **Backs to the River** could give morale and combat efficiency when retreat is unavailable or the army is outnumbered. A second skill, **False Crossing**, could place a visible decoy objective while allowing another formation to redeploy with reduced detection. His counter should be good reconnaissance, control of supply routes and refusal to chase bait.

**Xiang Yu — the shock commander**

Xiang Yu is almost the inverse of Han Xin. At Julu, historical tradition associates him with the famous decision to destroy the means of retreat and sharply limit supplies before attacking a stronger Qin force—later memorialized by the expression “break the cauldrons and sink the boats.” His victory established tremendous military prestige, and accounts of his career consistently emphasize aggressive battlefield effectiveness. citeturn17search0turn17search9

He continued to win battles during the Chu–Han struggle, but battlefield superiority did not translate into political victory. After the collapse of Qin he attempted to structure the conquered realm through a group of kingdoms under his hegemonic supremacy; revolts quickly destabilized the settlement. Historical accounts also criticize his handling of talented advisers and subordinates and emphasize acts of extreme violence, including the killing of surrendered Qin troops in the traditional Julu aftermath. Such violence could encourage enemies to resist rather than trust surrender. Eventually Liu Bang's broader coalition and personnel system overcame Xiang Yu despite the latter's formidable battlefield record. citeturn17search0turn17search9

That produces an excellent game character: **enormous immediate combat power, morale shock, intimidation and offensive initiative, but terrible political sustainability**.

**Game translation:** **Break the Cauldrons** could remove retreat and sharply increase attack, charge and morale for several turns. **Hegemon of Western Chu** could make nearby friendly troops nearly immune to rout after Xiang Yu personally enters combat. The cost should be severe: diplomatic relations deteriorate when coercive options are used, enemy surrender probability falls if the player develops a reputation for massacres, and talented subordinate loyalty is difficult to maintain. That last feature is much more interesting than merely giving Xiang Yu “–20 Diplomacy.”

Han Xin should beat Xiang Yu through space, information and coalition warfare; Xiang Yu should beat Han Xin if he succeeds in forcing an immediate, concentrated close battle.

**Napoleon Bonaparte — the operational concentration specialist**

Napoleon's central gameplay identity should be **operational art and rapid concentration**, rather than a generic “European artillery buff.” Modern military scholarship debates precisely how to classify Napoleon within the history of operational art, but his campaigns clearly demonstrated the military value of semi-independent corps capable of marching along separate routes and concentrating for battle. U.S. Army professional studies describe corps structures combining multiple divisions with supporting capabilities and emphasize the operational significance of Napoleonic organization. citeturn10search0turn10search2

The 1805–07 sequence illustrates the system at its peak: French forces achieved the Ulm and Austerlitz victories in 1805, followed by Jena-Auerstedt in 1806 and Friedland in 1807, temporarily establishing French predominance on the continent. citeturn18view4

His weakness was not an inability to win battles but the increasing difficulty of turning repeated campaigns into a stable strategic settlement. Britain's naval superiority survived Trafalgar, pushing Napoleon toward the Continental System, which required extensive enforcement and generated economic and political resistance. Later wars exposed the costs of enormous distances, partisan warfare, hostile populations and overstretched communications. Research associated with the Fondation Napoléon notes the exhausting marching and countermarching of later campaigns, harassment of communications, Spanish and Portuguese resistance, and the growing political problems generated by continued imperial expansion. citeturn18view4turn10search13turn10search15

The Russian campaign particularly highlights the mismatch between operational brilliance and strategic logistics: Russian withdrawal, destruction or denial of resources, distance, attrition and the enormous demands of moving a huge army progressively eroded French strength. citeturn10search1

**Game translation:** **March Separately, Fight Together** lets several corps move faster on parallel roads without a large-army movement penalty, then converge on a battle within a limited radius. **Central Position** provides initiative when fighting several separated enemy armies. Napoleon's counter mechanic should be **Imperial Overextension**: every additional theater beyond the core logistical network increases supply consumption, partisan activity and reinforcement delay.

This makes him devastating during short campaigns near developed supply networks but progressively less efficient if the player tries to conquer everything without pauses.

**Lý Thường Kiệt — the preemptive-defense commander**

Lý Thường Kiệt's strongest design identity comes from the Song–Lý war. Vietnamese historical accounts associate him with the 1075–76 offensive against Song frontier positions, including Yongzhou, Qinzhou and Lianzhou, followed by the defense against the Song counter-invasion along the Như Nguyệt River system. He had also participated in the 1069 campaign against Champa and subsequently held important frontier responsibilities. citeturn12search23turn12search0turn12search9

That sequence makes him highly unusual: **his offense is part of his defense**. Instead of waiting for a larger neighbor to complete preparations, he attacks staging infrastructure and then falls back onto prepared defensive geography. In game terms, that is far more distinctive than portraying him merely as a high-defense general. citeturn12search23

The poem *Nam Quốc Sơn Hà* is traditionally associated with the resistance and with Lý Thường Kiệt, but authorship should not be treated as definitively proven; a historically responsible game can present the association as tradition rather than certainty. citeturn12search3

**Game translation:** **Preempt the Invasion** grants a temporary movement and siege bonus against an opponent that is mobilizing near the frontier. Destroying depots or military infrastructure reduces the strength of its eventual invasion. **Như Nguyệt Line** gives exceptional defense, morale and ranged effectiveness behind rivers or prepared fieldworks. His weakness is duration: the preemptive strategy should be excellent for disrupting and forcing negotiation, but less suited to indefinite occupation deep inside a large enemy empire.

**Trần Hưng Đạo — the defense-in-depth and logistics master**

Trần Hưng Đạo is the strongest candidate in this roster for a commander built around **strategic patience**. In the Mongol-Yuan invasions, the Trần state could temporarily abandon important territory rather than allow its field army to be destroyed. In 1285 the Yuan initially achieved major advances and occupied the capital region, yet Trần forces recovered and drove the invasion out. citeturn1search8

The 1287–88 invasion makes logistics particularly important. Vietnamese historical research summarized by the National Museum of History describes a large Yuan naval and supply operation; Trần Khánh Dư destroyed an important supply fleet, worsening the invaders' food problem. Trần Hưng Đạo then prepared the Bạch Đằng system for the withdrawing enemy. The final river battle exploited waterways, tides, blocking forces and prepared obstacles to turn the Yuan withdrawal into a major defeat. citeturn11search12turn11search6

The lesson is broader than “stake trap.” His real strategic package is **preserve the army → deny supplies → stretch the invader → identify the moment of maximum vulnerability → counterattack on selected terrain**. citeturn11search12turn1search8

His apparent weakness can therefore become excellent gameplay counterplay. He should not be the strongest commander in a surprise field battle on neutral ground. His peak effectiveness requires intelligence, preparation, favorable terrain and time. A player who forces him into an immediate unprepared engagement should gain an advantage; a player who lets him shape the campaign should probably lose.

**Game translation:** **Deny the Granaries** causes evacuated regions to yield fewer supplies to invaders while preserving part of their value for the defending faction. **Bạch Đằng Killing Ground** allows a prepared river sector to become increasingly lethal under the correct tide or timing condition. **Preserve the Army** reduces morale penalties from voluntarily abandoning cities—a very powerful mechanic that captures the historical willingness to exchange space for time.

**Nguyễn Huệ / Quang Trung — the tempo and surprise specialist**

Nguyễn Huệ's most famous campaign provides perhaps the cleanest “blitz” archetype in the entire roster. After Qing forces entered northern Vietnam, Nguyễn Huệ became emperor as Quang Trung and advanced north. Cambridge historical scholarship describes the Vietnamese counteroffensive as a surprise attack around the Lunar New Year in 1789; the Qing invasion was rapidly driven back. citeturn15search4turn15search13

Narrative military accounts describe a fast advance organized along multiple axes and employing infantry together with cavalry, elephants and firearms/artillery. Exact tactical details and troop figures vary by source, so the game should emphasize the well-supported principles—**speed, surprise, coordinated columns and decisive concentration**—rather than presenting every colorful numerical tradition as certain fact. citeturn2search3turn15search13

The clearest strategic limitation is not that Nguyễn Huệ lacked battlefield ability. Rather, his political-military system proved much less durable after his death in 1792; the Tây Sơn regime fell to Nguyễn Ánh roughly a decade later. It would be misleading to call that simply Nguyễn Huệ's “personal weakness,” but it demonstrates that exceptional personal command had not produced an equally resilient succession and institutional system. citeturn15search0turn15search19

**Game translation:** **Tết Thunderbolt** temporarily suppresses enemy reconnaissance/readiness and gives huge first-contact bonuses during a surprise offensive. **Five Columns** rewards simultaneous attacks from multiple routes into the same operational zone. His army should lose some advantage if the war becomes a static, multi-year struggle. In campaign mode, a **Personal Momentum** mechanic can make his faction exceptionally powerful while he is actively commanding but create a succession challenge if institutions have not been upgraded.

That distinction makes Nguyễn Huệ a radically different commander from Nguyễn Ánh: **Nguyễn Huệ wins through tempo; Nguyễn Ánh wins through persistence.**

## Rulers, state-builders and strategic monarchs

**Lê Thánh Tông — the institutional military monarch**

Lê Thánh Tông's reign is associated with far-reaching administrative, legal, educational and military reorganization. Research on his territorial and defense policies emphasizes maintaining a strong military and protecting frontiers, while academic treatments of the period note the strengthening and reorganization of the armed forces. The expansion of the Lê state culminated in the major 1471 campaign against Champa, after which Đại Việt's position along the eastern mainland Southeast Asian littoral became substantially stronger. citeturn1search0turn1search7turn15search19

His distinguishing strength should therefore be **the ability to convert governance into military power**. He is not primarily interesting as an individual battlefield duelist. His power comes from censuses, officials, legal order, military organization, education and a centralized state capable of sustained mobilization. citeturn1search0turn1search4

The 1471 destruction of the major Cham political center should also be presented carefully. It was a violent conquest with profound consequences for Champa, not simply a neutral “territory unlocked” moment. A historical game can represent expansion while also imposing displacement, integration and resistance costs rather than turning conquest into an uncomplicated bonus. citeturn15search19

**Game translation:** **Hồng Đức Institutions** improves taxation, recruitment quality, examinations and provincial control simultaneously. **Organized Mobilization** converts developed provinces into larger, more reliable armies. His counterbalance should be that much of his power is **state-dependent**: undeveloped or newly conquered territory provides fewer benefits and requires administrative investment. In a tactical battle mode, he should rank below Han Xin, Trần Hưng Đạo or Nguyễn Huệ; in a grand-strategy mode, he may be one of the strongest characters in the entire roster.

**Minh Mạng / Minh Mệnh — the centralizer**

The `minh_menh` folder is best interpreted as **Emperor Minh Mạng**, associated with the era-name rendering Minh Mệnh in historical references. His reign was characterized by major centralizing administrative reforms. The Nguyễn government replaced large regional structures with a more uniform provincial administration and strengthened vertical imperial oversight. citeturn2search11turn2search23

That centralization was particularly significant in the south, where the early Nguyễn monarchy had inherited powerful regional networks. Scholarship on the Lê Văn Khôi rebellion places it in the context of Minh Mạng's efforts to assert much stronger central authority over the southern provinces. citeturn4search15

His reign also involved coercive incorporation and cultural-political pressure in former Cham territories; academic research on the 1830s treats the final destruction or forced assimilation of the surviving Campā political order as part of this period. Internal rebellions such as that associated with Phan Bá Vành likewise required substantial military suppression. citeturn4search12turn5search16

This gives a much more nuanced game profile than simply “good administrator.” **Centralization improves efficiency but increases resistance among populations and regional elites whose autonomy is being removed.**

**Game translation:** **Provincial Reform** replaces semi-autonomous regional structures with standardized provinces, sharply increasing revenue, recruitment and direct control. **Imperial Inspection** reduces corruption and local-governor independence. The cost is a growing **Centralization Pressure** value: rapid reform in culturally or politically distinct regions raises unrest and rebellion risk.

Minh Mạng should be extremely strong at internal administration but relatively poor at soft-power integration compared with Nguyễn Phúc Chu or Trần Nhân Tông. That contrast creates useful faction diversity without requiring simplistic good/bad judgments.

**Nguyễn Ánh / Gia Long — the survivor and system-builder**

Nguyễn Ánh's career is defined by an extraordinary ability to survive reversals. As the surviving Nguyễn claimant during the Tây Sơn era, he spent decades fighting for restoration before ultimately defeating the Tây Sơn and founding the Nguyễn dynasty as Emperor Gia Long in 1802. citeturn2search5turn15search13

He made effective use of southern bases, fortifications, naval forces and foreign technical expertise. Cambridge scholarship notes the presence of French and other foreign personnel who helped train troops and develop Nguyễn military capabilities, while Nguyễn Ánh's recapture and development of Saigon gave him an important fortified center. Scholarship on the early Nguyễn dynasty also stresses how central the military remained to the construction of the new regime. citeturn15search13turn4search7turn15search3

This is why he should not be designed as a weaker version of Nguyễn Huệ. Their advantages are almost opposites:

| Nguyễn Huệ | Nguyễn Ánh |
|---|---|
| Wins quickly | Survives long enough to win |
| Surprise | Preparation |
| Operational tempo | Logistics |
| Decisive campaign | Cumulative reconstruction |
| Personal command peak | Institutional consolidation |
| Strong early battle swing | Strong comeback and late game |

Nguyễn Ánh's costs should appear in economic and political terms. The long warfare and subsequent state construction demanded heavy mobilization and revenue, while scholarship on the Gia Long state discusses substantial fiscal extraction. citeturn4search22

**Game translation:** **Return from Defeat** allows a destroyed field army to regenerate cadres more efficiently as long as the faction retains a secure coastal/base province. **Southern Arsenal** progressively improves naval, artillery and fortification technology through investment and foreign expertise. **Patient Restoration** increases bonuses after each failed campaign rather than resetting the faction completely.

His natural weakness is early-game momentum: he should need ports, money, time and protected bases. Nguyễn Huệ can destroy him if given the decisive battle he wants; Nguyễn Ánh becomes terrifying if he is repeatedly allowed to escape and rebuild.

**Nguyễn Phúc Chu — the frontier and maritime ruler**

Nguyễn Phúc Chu ruled the southern Nguyễn domain from the late seventeenth into the early eighteenth century. His reign sits inside the larger process through which the Nguyễn polity expanded southward while participating in maritime commerce and governing an increasingly diverse population. Research on Nguyễn Cochinchina emphasizes the rapid expansion, commercial connections and social diversity of the southern realm. citeturn4search4turn4search24

His diplomacy extended to European commercial actors: in 1703 he communicated with the English East India Company seeking cooperation or amicable relations. His reign also saw further Nguyễn pressure on and incorporation of Cham territory, including the political transformation of Pāṇḍuraṅga/Thuận Thành. citeturn4search0turn4search12

Religious patronage is another useful differentiator. Scholarship notes Nguyễn Phúc Chu's invitation to the Chinese Buddhist monk Shilian Dashan, illustrating the ruler's use of Buddhist networks and religious legitimacy. citeturn2search2

**Game translation:** Nguyễn Phúc Chu should be a **frontier-network character**, not a battle monster. **Southern Frontier** makes settlement, trading ports and newly incorporated territories cheaper to develop. **Maritime Envoys** increases foreign trade, access to specialists and diplomatic options. **Buddhist Patronage** can increase legitimacy and cultural influence.

The downside is **Frontier Complexity**: rapid expansion produces provinces with different political traditions and identities, which require negotiated integration. This creates a useful middle path between Minh Mạng's centralized control and Nguyễn Huệ's military conquest.

**Trần Nhân Tông — the unity and resilience king**

Trần Nhân Tông's reign coincided with the great Mongol-Yuan invasions of the 1280s. Academic treatment describes him as a successful ruler during the defeat of the Mongol threat, while Vietnamese historical sources connect his leadership to the Bình Than and Diên Hồng political-military councils used to strengthen elite and broader commitment to resistance. citeturn13search2turn13search0turn13search15

His military identity should nevertheless remain distinct from Trần Hưng Đạo. Trần Hưng Đạo is the campaign commander; Trần Nhân Tông is the monarch who **maintains political cohesion, legitimacy and willingness to continue the war**. That separation allows both characters to coexist without duplicating one another.

After the invasions, the monarchy faced severe material destruction. Historical accounts describe tax relief, amnesties and efforts at recovery, while Trần diplomacy sought a workable postwar relationship with the Yuan rather than permanent confrontation. citeturn13search20turn13search24

After abdication, Trần Nhân Tông became closely associated with Trúc Lâm Buddhism. UNESCO describes the Trúc Lâm tradition and its connection to the “King-Buddha” Trần Nhân Tông, emphasizing its synthesis of religious and indigenous intellectual traditions and its later cultural significance. citeturn13search1

**Game translation:** **Diên Hồng Consensus** raises national morale, reduces surrender pressure and temporarily converts elite faction approval into manpower. **King-Buddha** becomes available later in the campaign, trading some direct political control for legitimacy, cultural unity and diplomatic influence. **Reconstruction Edict** accelerates recovery of war-damaged provinces.

His weakness should be **limited personal tactical specialization**. He becomes strongest when paired with elite commanders—especially Trần Hưng Đạo. That produces a deliberate synergy: Nhân Tông keeps the state from collapsing; Hưng Đạo destroys the invasion army.

**Trịnh Cương — the fiscal administrator**

Trịnh Cương belongs in a very different category. During the Lê–Trịnh period, the Lê emperor formally remained sovereign while the Trịnh lords constructed a powerful parallel governing structure. Academic research describes how the Trịnh administration incorporated literati while preserving influential military and financial offices and developed a fiscal-military bureaucracy centered on institutions such as the Lục Phiên and Lục Cung. citeturn18view0

The same scholarly literature specifically identifies research on **Trịnh Cương and Nguyễn Công Hãng's financial reorganization in eighteenth-century northern Vietnam** and notes that under Trịnh Cương there were efforts both to restrain the authority of major military officials and to strengthen supervision over them. citeturn18view0

This makes Trịnh Cương ideal as an **economic-control character**. He should not be artificially converted into a great warrior merely because other characters are generals.

**Game translation:** **Fiscal Reorganization** increases effective tax collection while reducing leakage to intermediate officials. **Supervise the Grand Defenders** weakens dangerous military governors and reduces coup risk. **Lục Phiên Administration** converts bureaucracy into military upkeep efficiency.

His structural weakness is extremely interesting: the Trịnh operated a powerful government while formally coexisting with the Lê monarchy. The Cambridge analysis explicitly characterizes the imperial administration as increasingly hollow while the Trịnh established a parallel de facto government. citeturn18view0 A game can model this as **Dual Legitimacy**: Trịnh Cương can exercise enormous effective power but cannot casually eliminate every ceremonial or institutional constraint without destabilizing the political order.

## Game-ready balance matrix

The numbers below are **design recommendations, not historical measurements**. They translate the evidence above into a ten-point strategy-game scale. “Battle” means immediate battlefield command; “Maneuver” means operational movement/deception; “Logistics” includes supply and long-war capability; “Governance” represents institutions and internal administration; “Diplomacy” covers coalition-building and peaceful integration; “Morale” represents the ability to sustain commitment; and “Resilience” measures effectiveness after setbacks.

| Character | Battle | Maneuver | Logistics | Governance | Diplomacy | Morale | Resilience |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| **Han Xin** | 10 | **10** | 8 | 4 | 4 | 9 | 7 |
| **Xiang Yu** | **10** | 9 | 5 | 4 | 3 | **10** | 6 |
| **Napoleon** | **10** | **10** | 8 | 8 | 6 | 9 | 7 |
| **Lý Thường Kiệt** | 9 | 9 | 8 | 7 | 7 | 9 | 9 |
| **Trần Hưng Đạo** | **10** | 9 | **10** | 7 | 7 | **10** | **10** |
| **Nguyễn Huệ** | **10** | **10** | 8 | 6 | 7 | **10** | 8 |
| **Lê Thánh Tông** | 7 | 7 | 9 | **10** | 8 | 8 | 9 |
| **Minh Mạng** | 5 | 5 | 8 | **10** | 4 | 7 | 7 |
| **Nguyễn Ánh** | 8 | 7 | **10** | 9 | 8 | 9 | **10** |
| **Nguyễn Phúc Chu** | 5 | 6 | 8 | 9 | 9 | 7 | 8 |
| **Trần Nhân Tông** | 7 | 7 | 8 | 9 | **10** | **10** | **10** |
| **Trịnh Cương** | 4 | 4 | 9 | 9 | 7 | 6 | 7 |

The important balance principle is that **10 does not mean “historically superior person”; it means “this is what the character should specialize in.”** Napoleon and Han Xin can both have Maneuver 10 while playing completely differently: Han Xin manipulates information and enemy decisions, whereas Napoleon manipulates corps positioning, operational concentration and tempo. Similarly, Nguyễn Ánh and Trần Hưng Đạo can both have Logistics 10, but Nguyễn Ánh uses logistics to regenerate and sustain a long restoration war while Hưng Đạo weaponizes logistics denial against an invading army. Their historical bases are different. citeturn18view2turn10search2turn11search12turn15search13

A particularly useful balance structure is to give every top-tier figure one **strategic failure condition**:

| Character | Peak condition | Natural counter / weakness |
|---|---|---|
| **Han Xin** | Open maneuver space, enemy misinformation | Political suspicion; secure enemy supply network |
| **Xiang Yu** | Immediate decisive battle | Coalition warfare, diplomacy, prolonged conflict |
| **Napoleon** | Several corps operating inside sustainable logistical radius | Strategic overextension, attrition, insurgency |
| **Lý Thường Kiệt** | Enemy invasion is detectable before completion | Long occupation far beyond prepared frontier |
| **Trần Hưng Đạo** | Time to prepare and deny enemy logistics | Sudden battle before defensive system is ready |
| **Nguyễn Huệ** | Surprise and operational momentum | Long static war; loss of initiative |
| **Lê Thánh Tông** | Developed centralized state | Expensive integration of newly conquered frontiers |
| **Minh Mạng** | Direct bureaucratic control | Regional resistance to aggressive centralization |
| **Nguyễn Ánh** | Secure bases plus time | Early destruction before rebuilding cycle begins |
| **Nguyễn Phúc Chu** | Trade-connected expanding frontier | Integration burden and frontier fragmentation |
| **Trần Nhân Tông** | Cohesive state with capable subordinate commanders | Limited direct tactical burst |
| **Trịnh Cương** | Stable bureaucracy and tax base | Dual-state legitimacy and administrative complexity |

This approach gives the player a reason to choose characters based on preferred strategy rather than merely selecting whoever has the largest numerical bonus.

## Signature skills, counters and faction synergies

A historically grounded skill system can go further than passive stat bonuses:

| Character | Signature skill | Secondary mechanic | Mandatory weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Han Xin** | **Backs to the River** — stronger when retreat is impossible | **False Crossing** — deploy a convincing maneuver decoy | **Court Suspicion** — excessive independent prestige threatens political survival |

Several combinations are especially strong for a game with commander-ruler pairings.

**Trần Nhân Tông + Trần Hưng Đạo** should be one of the most powerful defensive synergies. Nhân Tông maintains political unity, manpower commitment and postwar recovery; Hưng Đạo converts that national resilience into operational defense, supply denial and decisive counterattack. This division corresponds well to their different historical roles during the Mongol-Yuan invasions. citeturn13search2turn13search0turn11search12

**Lê Thánh Tông + an elite field commander** should transform high Governance into unusually efficient armies. His historical strength lies heavily in state organization, military reform and mobilization rather than requiring him to outperform specialist commanders tactically. citeturn1search0turn1search7

**Nguyễn Huệ versus Nguyễn Ánh** should produce perhaps the best asymmetrical rivalry in the roster. Nguyễn Huệ has the advantage when the campaign can be decided through speed, shock and rapid operational concentration; Nguyễn Ánh becomes more dangerous with every month he is allowed to preserve bases, accumulate naval/logistical resources and reconstruct forces. Their historical contest eventually ended with Nguyễn Ánh founding the Nguyễn dynasty after Nguyễn Huệ's death and the subsequent weakening of Tây Sơn power. citeturn15search0turn15search13

**Han Xin versus Xiang Yu** can perform a similar function for the Chinese scenario. Xiang Yu should dominate direct shock confrontation; Han Xin should dominate the wider operational puzzle. The historical outcome of the Chu–Han conflict strongly supports such differentiation without needing to portray Xiang Yu as a bad battlefield commander—he emphatically was not. citeturn17search0turn17search9turn18view2

**Napoleon versus Trần Hưng Đạo** would make an interesting ahistorical sandbox matchup precisely because their systems attack one another's strengths. Napoleon wants rapid corps convergence and a decisive engagement; Hưng Đạo wants to refuse that engagement, remove supplies, extend communications and counterattack after attrition. This comparison is a game-design inference from their historically documented operational patterns, not a claim about who would have “really won.” citeturn10search2turn10search13turn11search12

## Historical cautions and final character hierarchy

Several famous stories should **not** be coded into the game as unquestionable facts.

For **Han Xin and Xiang Yu**, dramatic ancient troop numbers—hundreds of thousands at individual engagements—come through an ancient historiographical tradition and should not be converted literally into unit ratios. What is safer to preserve is the operational pattern: Han Xin's deception at Jingxing and other campaigns, Xiang Yu's aggressive victory at Julu, and the contrast between Xiang Yu's battlefield performance and Han/Liu Bang's superior long-term political outcome. citeturn8search38turn17search0turn18view2

For **Lý Thường Kiệt**, *Nam Quốc Sơn Hà* can appear as a morale event or cultural artifact, but the UI should say **“traditionally attributed to”** rather than declaring authorship certain. His historically stronger gameplay foundation is the combination of the 1075–76 preemptive campaign and the subsequent Như Nguyệt defense. citeturn12search3turn12search23

For **Trần Hưng Đạo**, Bạch Đằng should not become a magical “stake attack” usable anywhere. The actual strategic significance lies in preparation, river conditions, enemy withdrawal, logistical crisis and coordination among Trần forces. Making the ability dependent on geography and setup is both more historically faithful and better game design. citeturn11search6turn11search12

For **Nguyễn Huệ**, sources strongly support the extraordinary speed and surprise of the 1789 counteroffensive, but colorful reconstructions of exact marching schedules, formation numbers and battlefield details should be treated cautiously when sources disagree. The safest core is the combination of rapid mobilization, Lunar-New-Year surprise and a decisive northward counterattack. citeturn15search4turn15search13

For **Lê Thánh Tông, Minh Mạng and Nguyễn Phúc Chu**, territorial expansion should carry human and political costs. Champa and its successor polities were not simply empty provinces awaiting incorporation. The game can preserve strategic expansion while depicting resistance, cultural integration, displacement and legitimacy as systems rather than presenting conquest as an uncomplicated heroic reward. citeturn15search19turn4search12

For **Napoleon**, the best historical representation avoids both extremes: neither an invincible tactical genius ruined entirely by weather nor a commander whose victories were mere mythology. His early imperial campaigns demonstrate extraordinary operational effectiveness, while the Continental System, Iberian resistance, Russian campaign and later coalition warfare show the mounting strategic costs of an expanding empire and increasingly difficult communications and manpower requirements. citeturn18view4turn10search1turn10search13turn10search15

For **Trịnh Cương**, the lack of famous cinematic battles is an advantage rather than a design problem. Scholarship on the Lê–Trịnh fiscal-military structure gives him a distinctive economic-administrative identity: he can be the player choice for someone who enjoys making an existing state machine dramatically more efficient rather than personally charging into battle. citeturn18view0

The most important design conclusion is that the roster should **not** be balanced by making everyone equally good at battle. Historically, its diversity is the asset. **Xiang Yu should frighten an army; Han Xin should outthink it; Napoleon should converge on it; Lý Thường Kiệt should disrupt it before it crosses the frontier; Trần Hưng Đạo should starve it; Nguyễn Huệ should hit it before it is ready; Nguyễn Ánh should survive it; Lê Thánh Tông should build the state that can replace it; Minh Mạng should control the provinces that supply it; Nguyễn Phúc Chu should expand the network that finances it; Trần Nhân Tông should keep society willing to support it; and Trịnh Cương should make the treasury capable of paying for it.** Those distinct strategic identities are both more faithful to the historical record and a much stronger foundation for asymmetric game design.
