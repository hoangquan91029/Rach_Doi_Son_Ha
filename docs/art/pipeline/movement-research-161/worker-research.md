## Primary research and practical interpretation

[Toon Boom: Walk Analysis](https://learn.toonboom.com/modules/walk-cycle-animation/topic/walk-analysis1) describes contact, passing, down and up positions: impact lowers the body, passing bends the advancing leg, and push-off extends the body before the opposite contact. It emphasizes the entire body, including arms/head. Apply these relationships in the ESE projected plane, rather than treating each slot as a leg swap. This page's indexed primary text was retrieved by search; direct opening returned403.

[Toon Boom: Creating the Tie Down Animation](https://docs.toonboom.com/help/harmony-22/essentials/paperless-animation/create-tie-down-animation.html) places on-model drawing and volume preservation between rough motion and finish, using neighboring drawings and real-time playback. The practical change is a complete on-model rough cycle before any expensive realistic repaint. Search returned its primary documentation text; direct opening returned404, so the live page availability is uncertain. We are adapting the drawing principle, not importing its software or a cutout workflow.

[Toon Boom: Design with Harmony](https://learn.toonboom.com/courses/design-with-harmony-advanced-and-premium) groups character proportions/model sheets/relative size with perspective and color-model work. For this project, one ESE model drawing and a single projected ground plane must govern head, ribcage, pelvis, far-side occlusion and sandal planes. No3D construction is required.

[Adobe: Animation Key Poses](https://www.adobe.com/uk/creativecloud/animation/discover/animation-key-poses.html) describes pose-to-pose planning and breakdowns between key action poses. Its relevant lesson is to establish coherent motion before filling frames; adding inbetweens cannot repair incompatible character builds.

[Animation Mentor: 2D Walk Cycles and Character Movement](https://www.animationmentor.com/product/2d-animation-walk-cycles-character-movement/) treats believable motion as connected whole-body mechanics. This is supporting principle-level evidence, not a tool prescription, a promise of ImageGen control, or a recommendation to buy training.

## Substantially changed finite approach

1. **Choose one real D0 directional master.** Record the selected source, camera and root. Draw a small on-model ESE construction sheet from observation: hat/head, shoulder span, ribcage, waist/pelvis, tunic hem, knee/wrap/sandal proportions. Use complete original drawing contours, never extracted pixels or textured limb pieces. Match body volumes to that master; do not preserve153's generic tall body.
2. **Author the entire eight-slot rough cycle before painting.** Contact/down/passing/up/opposite contact/down/passing/up. Draw torso, pelvis, legs, arms and clothing together in every slot. Maintain one perspective ground plane, stable near/far identity and ordinary walking knee clearance. Stance knee softens on impact; pelvis shifts over support; chest counters pelvis; arm swing passes naturally. Existing contacts may survive only if their whole-body relationship works with this new construction. Do not force a crooked rough cycle to retain them.
3. **Tie down at gameplay size.** Play the original complete drawings at proposed unchanged runtime cadence with the single family transform. One brief look should answer: same person throughout, believable weight transfer, no tall/narrow collapse, sensible foot arc and closing seam. Correct visible whole-body problems in the original drawings. Onion/reference overlays are diagnostic drawings, not exported source warps or perframe registration. Minor cloth detail stays irrelevant.
4. **Change the repaint contract.** After rough-cycle intake and a new execution order, propose ONE builtin trial repaint of the hardest passing slot, zero retries. Make the chosen actual D0 master the identity/edit authority; attach the on-model complete target drawing as pose authority and the whole rough-cycle sheet as temporal context. Explicitly preserve master body mass and projected proportions. The tool must paint one coherent whole character, not trace the generic guide as final anatomy. This differs from155's isolated primary vector target plus two contact-only appearance references.
5. **Use the one returned illustration as a capability test.** Compare it once to the master and approved rough neighbors at89px. If torso/hip mass changes, leg linkage collapses or camera drifts, stop this method trial; do not open opposite passing or inbetween calls and do not revise prompt endlessly. A returned realistic texture is not success.
6. **Only after that test passes, create a new bounded continuation.** At most one opposite-passing wholeframe, zero retry; then a separately dispatched finite set of remaining frames bound to actual selected neighbors and their approved whole-pose drawings. Build the actual full loop and judge it briefly. Independent motion/root intake precedes runtime and fanout. A one-frame success does not prove temporal consistency.

## Limitations and honest stopping point

Built-in ImageGen accepts visual references but does not expose guaranteed geometry, exact canvas placement, or temporal identity locks. Changing reference roles and improving drawings reduces ambiguity; it cannot guarantee a usable frame. References are also not motion capture. The eight-slot plan is a practical stylization of walking, so exact foot lock requires subsequent ordinary gameplay movement evidence.

If the new single-call test still changes the character's build, the credible next alternative is skilled full-frame2D redraw/paint over an approved complete rough cycle, with one finished illustration per slot. That needs a real illustration capability or human artist; this research does not pretend the agent has already delivered it. No cutout assembly, source-pixel splicing,3D, API/CLI fallback or hidden retries.
