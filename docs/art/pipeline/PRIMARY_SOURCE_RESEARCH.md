# Deterministic villager animation: production decision note

**Prepared:** 2026-10-08  
**Scope:** Original RTS unit visuals for the Rạch Đôi Sơn Hà project.  
**Current direction:** Prefer a deterministic, complete 3D character pipeline whose final deliverables are flattened rendered frames. Author at **192–256 px per source frame**, inspect at the intended **64–96 px on-screen size**. This is the current parent scope, not a claim about the cited games' technical specifications.

## Recommendation

Use one locked 3D character, rig, materials, animation set, dimetric camera, light rig, and render script as the source of truth. Render complete transparent cels offline for each required direction and action; the game should display those cels as ordinary sprites, not deform a runtime 3D model or use runtime bones. Keep 2D paintover/compositing as a bounded hybrid correction stage for details that do not survive downsampling, not as a second competing character pipeline.

This approach best addresses the current bottleneck: limb-by-limb art has produced inconsistent anatomy and part proportions, while the approved character is a coherent full-body image with important asymmetric costume details. A complete 3D render keeps the whole silhouette, costume, hands, and occlusion together for every cel. It also makes a change to the model or pose reproducible across all rendered views. It costs more up front because the model, rig, materials, and camera need approval before large-scale rendering.

The 3D source should follow the locked game's dimetric view, then be tested at native target size. The existing identity metadata records a 1254×1254 canvas, 2:1 dimetric/tile ratio, 54×27 tile, 26.565° elevation, and intended pivot (654,1140). The visible full body occupies about x=399–858, y=52–1181 at alpha>128; the measured sandal contact is around y=1179, about 39 px below the intended pivot. These facts come from the identity lock and metadata, not from the art-method comparison. The reference itself shows a front-facing villager in a woven nón lá, pale patterned tunic, asymmetric dark sash, loose dark trousers, leg wraps, and sandals, with a finely textured, painterly finish. At 64–96 px on screen, much of that surface texture will collapse; silhouette, broad value/color regions, hat/sash asymmetry, hand/tool readability, and foot contact need priority over micro-detail.

Use 8 directions because the project master explicitly requires N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW. The animation frame count per action, per-unit exceptions, exact timing, and any approved mirroring exception are **not yet specified** by the reviewed project contract. Choose frame counts only after the first complete render pilot is reviewed at 64–96 px and timing is tested in the actual game. Do not infer those counts from Age of Empires or StarCraft references. Do not blindly mirror asymmetric units; for this project, treat mirroring as disallowed unless a later, unit-specific symmetry review explicitly allows it.

## What the reference games verify—and what they do not

The current Age of Empires II: Definitive Edition release notes show a production model built around angle-specific sprite assets and validation. Official notes mention conversion from SLP to SMX, validate `.smx` and `.sld` mod sprites at startup, disable unreadable/corrupt sprite mods, and allow sprite variations for buildings that have several graphic angles. The latest release note found during this review, Update 185872 (22 September 2026), adds ship animations and fixes a corrupted Siege Tower destruction frame; an earlier 2026 update fixes a graphic display problem on certain unit angles. This supports the practical value of named, validated per-view/per-action cels and content checks. It does **not** establish the full direction count, per-action frame counts, or mirroring rules for every AoE II unit. [AoE II DE update 34055](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/aoe2de-update-34055/), [update 83607](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/age-of-empires-ii-definitive-edition-update-83607/), [update 99311](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/age-of-empires-ii-definitive-edition-update-99311/), [update 169123](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/age-of-empires-ii-definitive-edition-update-169123/), [latest checked update 185872](https://www.ageofempires.com/news/age-of-empires-ii-definitive-edition-update-185872/).

Blizzard describes StarCraft: Remastered as preserving unit identity and silhouettes while redrawing/updating graphics for 4K, with core gameplay unchanged. A senior artist describes silhouette retention as a recognition requirement. This is a useful precedent for making the higher-resolution render read like the same unit at game zoom. The article is an art-direction account, not a sprite-format manual; it does not verify a general direction count, frame count, or flip policy. [Remastering StarCraft’s Art](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/20695698/remastering-starcraft-s-art), [Behind the Scenes of StarCraft: Remastered](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/20726732/behind-the-scenes-of-starcraft-remastered).

The project master is the authority for this game's intended eight directions and state list. It names idle, walk, attack, hit, and death, with gather, carry, build, repair, reload, special ability, mount, and unmount as additional states where needed. It also says not to mirror a sprite blindly when asymmetric historical details would be wrong. The reviewed v4 identity contract states that the locked villager view must not be mirrored. These project statements are separate from the games' official release notes.

## Comparison across nine production criteria

The table compares likely outcomes for this project. Ratings are qualitative engineering judgments, not benchmark measurements: **Strong** means the method naturally supports the criterion; **Conditional** means it can work with specific controls or added labor; **Weak** means the method works against the criterion or incurs a large cost.

| Criterion | Independent frames | Runtime 2D rig | Baked 2D rig | Prerendered 3D cels | Hybrid 3D + 2D finish |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Identity and continuity across frames | **Conditional:** each frame can be art-directed, but hands, face, costume and proportions can drift across frames and views. | **Strong:** shared parts preserve clothing and proportions; joint deformation can expose seams or stretch features. | **Strong:** one rig yields coherent sequences; errors in the rig are repeated in every baked frame. | **Strong:** one model/material rig and camera preserve proportions and occlusion across rendered frames; bad model choices repeat consistently. | **Strong/Conditional:** 3D anchors identity; hand paintovers or overlays can introduce style and scale mismatch. |
| 2. View coverage and depth/occlusion | **Strong if fully authored:** every view can be composed correctly, but each must be made and checked separately. | **Weak/Conditional:** planar layering and hand-authored depth order do not provide true volume; multiple view-specific rigs may be required. | **Conditional:** good for a fixed set of 2D views; the flat rig cannot reveal unseen surfaces automatically. | **Strong:** actual volume, camera rotation, and depth-tested overlap produce each view from the same character. | **Strong:** 3D resolves the base view and depth; 2D overlays can still fail at a view-specific overlap. |
| 3. Silhouette and style at 64–96 px | **Strong per cel:** direct control over every small-size silhouette; high labor to keep all frames aligned. | **Conditional:** clear at small size if joints are well placed; rotations can make flat limbs look thin, broad, or disconnected. | **Strong/Conditional:** offline correction is possible before flattening, but the rig still limits poses and silhouettes. | **Conditional:** physically coherent silhouettes, but materials, lighting, thin props, and fine texture must be tuned for downsampling. | **Strong potential:** correction can restore line weight and key detail; needs strict consistency rules. |
| 4. Motion and action range | **Strong in principle:** any pose can be drawn; transitions and in-betweens are expensive. | **Strong for ordinary motions:** fast to animate once rigged; difficult actions may need custom meshes or extra parts. | **Strong:** offline keyframes, mesh warps, and cleanup; each new state still must be authored and baked. | **Strong:** full-body deformation, contact, weapon/tool handling, and camera motion are available if the rig is good. | **Strong:** 3D motion plus selected 2D corrections; the integration step increases complexity. |
| 5. Up-front cost | **Low for one concept cel; high for a complete set.** | **Medium/high:** cutout/mesh setup, joints, pivots, depth order, and corrective poses. | **High:** rig plus offline animation, render automation, and review. | **Very high:** model, topology, rig, materials, lighting, animation, camera, and render pipeline. | **Highest:** all 3D setup plus a paintover/compositing convention and per-cel exception handling. |
| 6. Marginal cost as classes × actions × directions grow | **Weak:** grows roughly with every required class/action/frame/view combination. | **Strong after rigging:** many frames are cheap; new body types and view exceptions still need rig work. | **Conditional:** reuse saves posing, but every requested frame/view creates another flattened image and QA item. | **Strong after the model and pipeline exist:** additional cels are renders from shared sources; new units still need their own models or approved variants. | **Conditional:** render scaling is good, but painted exceptions multiply with frames and directions. |
| 7. Runtime cost and asset footprint | **Low runtime; potentially high atlas/storage footprint.** | **Medium runtime:** transform/mesh work and rig data are evaluated at runtime; low flattened-image count. | **Low runtime:** ordinary image/atlas draws; many raster frames consume storage/texture memory. | **Low runtime if delivered as cels:** same sprite draw path; storage grows with flattened frames and directions. | **Low runtime if all corrections are baked;** separate runtime overlays/effects add draw/state cost. |
| 8. Determinism and QA | **High for fixed source images; low if each cell is generated independently without locked inputs.** | **High:** same rig, pose, and time reproduce the same image, subject to renderer/version controls. | **High:** fixed rig and bake settings yield reviewable cels; pin bake tool and inputs. | **High:** pinned model, animation, camera, lights, materials, renderer, and settings can reproduce complete cels. Validate byte/pixel stability in the chosen renderer. | **Medium/high:** repeatable if overlays and render parameters are versioned; more stages create more failure points. |
| 9. Change cost and long-term maintenance | **Weak:** local edits are easy, global corrections and consistency sweeps are expensive. | **Strong/Conditional:** one rig edit can improve many motions; a bad shared edit can break every motion. | **Conditional:** source rig is maintainable; affected cels must be rebaked, reviewed, and re-atlased. | **Strong/Conditional:** master assets support global fixes and rerendering; requires keeping the 3D project, versions, and render script healthy. | **Conditional:** flexible for difficult details, but 3D and paintover sources must stay synchronized and traceable. |

## Per-class policy and risks

| Class | Preferred base | What to keep in 3D before baking | Principal risk / required gameplay-size check |
|---|---|---|---|
| Villagers and civilians | Shared civilian rig family; render complete cels. | Face, hat, tunic, sash, legs, hands, tool grip, foot contact, and carried loads. Use material/prop variants only where silhouette remains legible. | Fine woven fabric and facial detail disappear at 64–96 px. Verify hat and sash identity, hand/tool separation, shadow/feet, and readable carry/gather/build actions. |
| Spearmen and melee infantry | Infantry rig with weapon/shield attachments; render per view. | Shield-side occlusion, spear length and tilt, grip changes, attack recovery, hit/death poses. | Spear tips and narrow limbs alias; shield may hide the body or switch sides incorrectly. Check all views and team-color readability; do not mirror asymmetric shields/ornaments. |
| Arquebusiers/musket infantry | Separate weapon-hand/action rig or a controlled infantry variant. | Two-hand support, aim, recoil, muzzle flash as a separate effect if useful, reload and recovery. | Weapon can dominate the sprite or pass through face/body; small reload motions may be unreadable. Review firing and reload at target size and action speed. |
| Cavalry | Paired mount-and-rider rig, rendered as one composite sprite per cel. | Horse body/legs, rider seat, reins, weapon, saddle, mount shadow, and contact. Keep rider and mount pose relationship deterministic. | Limb tangling and rider bounce; rider may disappear against mount; movement cadence must match game speed. Inspect full silhouette, turning, attack, and death from each view. |
| Artillery and wagons | 3D vehicle/chassis rig; separately attach crew when present. | Wheel rotation, chassis yaw, barrel elevation, recoil, draw/haul pose, crew attachment and shadows. | Wheels may slide; barrel/crew overlap may be unreadable; turning radius can look wrong in a single-plane sprite. Check stationary fire, travel, turn, impact, and destruction. |
| Ships and transports | 3D hull, deck, mast/sail, and occupant rig; bake cels, with water/wake as a separable effect layer. | Hull orientation, waterline, sail state, deck silhouettes, wake origin, shoreline/depth relationships. | Transparent cels can lose water contact; wakes can imply motion when stopped; high views can make hull direction ambiguous. Review on actual water and shoreline backgrounds. |
| King, general, and historical leaders | Individual 3D models on the shared camera/render standard; unique materials and silhouette per leader. | Face/headwear, armor/clothing asymmetry, weapon, cape, and role-specific action/death. | Shared rig/model variants may flatten historical identity. Check recognition at gameplay size, side ownership, asymmetric costume details, and general-status readability. |

## Production and review gates

1. Build one complete villager model/rig and one deterministic render script. The input package must identify the model, rig, animation/action, view, camera, lights, materials, render engine/version, resolution, output alpha, frame index, and pivot.
2. Render a short complete pilot in all eight project directions. The project specifies the directions, but the exact frames per action are still open. Include a neutral frame and representative walk/gather/build or tool-action frames only after the timing and action brief are settled.
3. Render at 192–256 px source resolution; inspect the actual final cels at 64 px, 80 px, and 96 px on screen against terrain, water, shadows, selection markers, and neighboring units. Do not judge only enlarged previews.
4. Approve silhouette, view mapping, ground contact, alpha fringe, palette/value blocks, weapon/tool read, and transition timing before expanding to more classes or actions. Keep source 3D project and flattened outputs versioned together.
5. If 2D paintover is used, limit it to identified cells/features, record those regions and source hashes, and rerun the same small-size review. Do not let hand-painted corrections become an untracked alternate source of anatomy.
6. Keep runtime animation simple: the runtime selects direction/action/frame and plays flattened cels with data-driven timing. Do not add runtime 3D or skeletal deform unless a later performance/feature decision reopens it.

## Verified facts vs. inference and unknowns

**Verified from the project files inspected:** the villager master is a 1254×1254 transparent South/front-facing image with an asymmetric sash and nón lá; the identity lock lists eight static views; its target view metadata records a dimetric 2:1 ratio, tile 54×27, elevation 26.565°, and intended pivot (654,1140); the project master requests eight character directions and the listed minimum/additional action states. The current parent scope prefers complete 3D renders as final flattened art at 192–256 px source and 64–96 px on screen.

**Verified from primary external sources:** Age II DE official updates refer to SLP→SMX conversion, SMX/SLD sprite validation, angle-related sprite variations, and angle-specific graphics fixes. Blizzard's StarCraft: Remastered art notes describe retaining unit identity/silhouettes while updating visual resolution, and retaining core gameplay. The ControlNet paper describes learned spatial conditioning (including edges, depth, segmentation, and pose) for pretrained diffusion models. The IP-Adapter paper describes image-prompt conditioning compatible with text and structural controls; it also reports that fine-grained image features can improve reference consistency while reducing diversity.

**Inference for this project:** complete 3D rendering should reduce cross-view anatomical drift and make occlusion, weapon/tool contact, and costume placement easier to keep consistent. Flattening the renders keeps runtime work close to sprite selection/drawing and gives an auditable image result. These benefits depend on a correct model/rig and a stable render pipeline; 3D alone does not ensure the approved painted look or low-resolution readability. 2D paintover may improve selected details but creates a second correction source.

**Limits of generative conditioning:** ControlNet can guide spatial structure, and IP-Adapter can condition on an image reference alongside text and structural controls. They are learned conditioning mechanisms, not a promise of exact identity, contour, pivot, frame-to-frame continuity, or deterministic sprite-cell output. The ControlNet paper itself notes that text-only spatial composition is difficult and can require trial-and-error; the IP-Adapter paper describes prior image-feature methods as only partially faithful and reports a quality/consistency/diversity tradeoff for finer-grained features. The conclusion that these tools should not be the final source of game-critical animation cels is a project-specific inference, not a finding that they cannot be useful for concepts or constrained drafts.

**Unknown or still to decide:**

- Exact final frame counts and timing for each action/state.
- Whether every unit class needs all eight directions for every state, or whether any state uses a reduced view set.
- Final sprite atlas layout, compression, and frame metadata format.
- Exact 3D camera implementation parameters (focal length/orthographic scale, target, clipping) and renderer settings; the lock records projection ratio/elevation/tile/pivot, not the complete camera rig.
- Any permitted mirroring exceptions. Default for this asymmetric villager and other asymmetric historical units: do not mirror unless explicitly reviewed and approved.
- Whether the 192–256 px source frame is square or tightly sized to the character, and the final padding policy. Keep pivot/ground contact stable; do not auto-crop or bbox-fit without approval.
- The target lighting/material recipe needed to translate the current richly textured painterly character into readable 64–96 px game sprites.
