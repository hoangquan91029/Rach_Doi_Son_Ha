# Game design

A match pairs one King with one General per side. Kill the opposing General to win; destruction of a residence is an economic setback rather than immediate defeat. Armies pay for time and movement with finite rations. Raids, terrain, crossings and supply transport provide alternatives to massing damage.

Resources: wood and iron become central strategic stock after delivery to a residence/depot; food remains inventory in buildings and wagons and is removed during recruitment/loading/resupply. Soldiers replenish reserves near stocked stores/wagons. Low supply slows and weakens troops; starvation adds morale pressure and delayed attrition.

Troops: villagers, infantry, spearmen (anti-cavalry), firearms (ranged), cavalry (mobility), artillery (siege), supply wagons, warships and transports. Buildings: residence, barracks, cavalry camp, artillery workshop, shipyard, fort, walls/gates, depot, granary. Only the King may replace a destroyed Main Residence; an unfinished replacement reserves the unique slot. Construction recovery/cancellation must remain usable and bounded.

Leadership: Twelve historical identities from the supplied dossier. Tags combine into reusable synergies, and each active ability has an actual timing/geography/cargo/production/morale effect. Numbers and mechanics are design interpretations, not historical measurements. This first implementation simplifies historical political weaknesses; they remain design/acceptance review items rather than new historical assertions.

Maps: delta with three ford corridors and a southern naval sea; coast with mountain passes, forests, northern river crossings and southern coastal routes. Terrain modifies movement/detection/range/defense. Unexplored and remembered fog shades are distinct. Enemy leaders are hidden without scouting.

AI: cadence and timing differ by difficulty; resource costs and damage rules remain shared with humans. It gathers, produces, builds, retreats/protects its General, transports food, and sends ordinary army attacks. Victory is assessed by the same simulation rules as humans.

Visual target: an angled 2.5D battlefield and readable parchment/jade UI. Original leader candidates are static eight-direction sources. Reusable procedural animation is a development tool. Full final unit/leader motion, historical readability, and finished-map playtesting remain required before COMPLETE.
